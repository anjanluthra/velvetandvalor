There is a particular smell to a working tannery in Tuscany: warm leather, oak bark, and the faint mineral note of spring water. It is not glamorous. But it is, in our view, where luxury actually begins — long before a case is cut, stitched, or fitted with gold.

Every Velvet & Valor case starts its life in the same Florentine tannery. Not a different supplier each season, not whichever mill quotes the lowest price — the same family-run house we have worked with from the beginning. Here is why that consistency matters more than almost anything else we do.

## The vegetable-tanned difference

Most leather goods you'll handle this year are chrome-tanned. It's fast, cheap, and produces a uniform, slightly plasticky hide in a single day. Our tannery does the opposite. They use **vegetable tanning** — a centuries-old method that soaks the hides in pits of natural tannins drawn from oak and chestnut bark.

It takes weeks. It cannot be rushed. And the result is leather that is denser, stronger, and — crucially — *alive*. Vegetable-tanned leather breathes, drinks in conditioning oils, and develops the deep, individual patina that we explain how to nurture in our [leather care guide](/blog/how-to-care-for-your-full-grain-leather-case).

> "You cannot fake age in leather. You can only choose a hide that will earn it."
> — Kate Luthra, Creative Director & Founder

## Why the *same* tannery, every time

Provenance is not a marketing word for us. Working with one tannery means:

- **Consistency of hand.** The grain, weight, and finish of every batch is something we know intimately. A case you buy next year will feel like the one you bought this year.
- **Traceability.** We know where the hides come from and how they were processed. That accountability is impossible when you change suppliers to chase margins.
- **A real relationship.** Decades of working together means our makers can request specific cuts, finishes, and tolerances that a transactional supplier would never accommodate.

## Provenance you can feel

When you hold a [Noble Steed case](/products/noble-steed), the things you notice — the suppleness, the weight, the way the edges are burnished rather than painted — all trace back to that one tannery. It is the quiet foundation beneath the artwork.

We could source leather more cheaply. We never will. Because the entire promise of Velvet & Valor — an object that looks better in five years than it does today — depends on getting this first, invisible step exactly right.
