From the Tack Room to the Show Ring: A Day in the Life

We spent a morning at a yard in the Cotswolds with three of our customers — riders who carry their Noble Steed Cases from early hacks to evening stables.

✦ From the Tack Room to the Show Ring: A Day in the Life

The yard is awake long before the rest of the world. We arrived at a livery in the Cotswolds at half past six on a grey spring morning to spend the day with three riders — a long-listed eventer, a dressage rider returning after maternity leave, and a teenager who lives, by her own account, "ninety percent at the stables." We wanted to see where a Velvet & Valor case actually lives. The answer, it turns out, is everywhere.

06:30 — The tack room

Coffee in gloved hands, breath fogging, the radio low. Phones come out constantly here: checking the forecast, the start times, the group chat of who's mucking out which row. This is the unglamorous heart of equestrian life, and it's where the difference between a flimsy case and a real one becomes obvious. Leather and gold don't mind cold fingers and hay dust.

09:00 — The hack

"I've dropped my phone in the school more times than I'll admit. The case has the scuffs to prove it — but honestly, it just looks better for them."

The patina we love so much is, in practice, a map of a rider's year. Every mark from a dropped phone or a muddy glove becomes part of the object's character rather than a flaw — exactly as full-grain leather is meant to age.

13:00 — The show ring

By early afternoon we'd moved to a local competition. Here the phone becomes mission control: warm-up times, video of the round for the trainer, the photo someone's mum takes at the prize-giving. A case here is on display as much as the rider is — which is rather the point of choosing one with artwork you actually love.

17:00 — Evening stables

The day ends as it began, in the quiet of the barn. Rugs on, haynets filled, one last scroll before the drive home. We asked each rider the same question: what makes a piece of kit worth keeping? The answers were almost identical — it has to survive the yard, and it has to mean something.

That is the brief we design to. A case that travels from the tack room to the show ring and back, gathering character rather than damage, carrying a name or a portrait of the horse that makes it unmistakably theirs.

We're grateful to the riders who let us follow them around all day. You can read the story behind the collection they carry, or find your own.
